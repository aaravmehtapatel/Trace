# Trace: the company

**Trace tells a fund with more companies than people which ones need a partner this week, and why, without asking founders for anything.**

## The problem
A pre-seed fund like Everywhere Ventures holds 265+ companies with a handful of partners. At pre-seed most founders don't send regular updates, and every portfolio tool (Carta, Standard Metrics, Visible) starts from what founders report. So the fund's picture of its own portfolio is a spreadsheet that's stale the day it's made. That spreadsheet feeds quarterly marks, LP letters and the pitch for the next fund. Partners learn a company is in trouble when it asks for a bridge, and learn about exits when a lawyer emails.

## What Trace does
Every month, for every company:
- **Free public checks:** the website and its archived history, email and domain records, GitHub, public job boards, SEC Form D.
- **Licensed headcount and news** from Akta (sourced from LinkedIn, never scraped).
- **A status with its evidence:** active, fading, quiet or no public activity, each linked to the records behind it. Where we can't confirm a company, we say so instead of guessing.
- **The outputs a partner acts on:** this month's raises, exits and headcount movers; a call list with the question to ask; a CSV for the fund's CRM.

## Proof from the first run (Everywhere Ventures, 24 Oct 2026)
- 265 companies; 230 placed. 153 active, 17 fading, 52 quiet, 8 with no public activity.
- **Last 30 days:** 7 companies raised about $145M (North.cloud's $60M Series B has no Form D, so funding news counts too). 3 exits found without anyone reporting them: M7 Health (acquired by Ascend Learning), Aerodome (now Flock Safety) and Narrato (now Typeface). 3 renames caught: Mr Yum became me&u, still 212 people.
- **The portfolio employs 9,958 people across 171 companies, up 17% in a year.** That's the kind of number funds put in LP letters and can't produce today.
- **Trace grades itself.** Against licensed headcount on 165 companies, free data's status was right 80% of the time where it was clear. Companies free data calls quiet are a coin flip (37% growing, 32% shrinking), so that's exactly where Trace leans on licensed data.
- **An investor at the event said they'd use it.**

## Unit economics (measured, not estimated)
- Licensed data for the whole first run: 460 Akta credits, about **$18, or 7 cents a company**. Public checks are free. A full run takes about 30 minutes, unattended.
- Proposed price: **$5 per portfolio company per month**. Everywhere: about $16K a year. A 2,000-company accelerator portfolio: about $120K a year.
- Data is under 2% of revenue at that price. The cost that matters is trust, and the accuracy panel is how Trace earns it.

## Who buys
1. **High-volume pre-seed funds and scout programs** (start): hundreds of companies, small teams, no updates. The pain is sharpest and the decision-maker is a partner.
2. **Accelerators and corporate venture programs:** thousands of alumni, where tracking status is a reporting duty to their own backers.
3. **LPs, fund-of-funds and secondary buyers** (expand): they can't get founder updates at all, and want to check what GPs report or price a stake. The same census, run across every fund they hold.

## Why now
- Licensed alternative data (headcount, news) is now cheap enough to run on every company in a portfolio: cents a company.
- Pre-seed portfolios got very large (300+ companies is normal for a "volume" fund), while their teams didn't grow.
- LPs are pushing on marks and distributions, so funds need a defensible view of what each company is worth today.

## Competition
- **Founder-reporting tools (Carta, Standard Metrics, Visible)** depend on data pre-seed founders don't send. Trace is complementary: it fills in the 80% who are silent.
- **Data platforms (Harmonic, Specter, PitchBook, Akta itself)** sell signals for finding deals. Trace is organized around a fund's own portfolio and turns signals into decisions with evidence, and it buys their data as an input.

## What's defensible
- **Company matching that's been tested against real errors:** namesakes (Otis Elevator vs Otis the vet startup), renames, redirects to acquirers, investment vehicles in SEC filings.
- **History that compounds:** each monthly run adds to a dated record per company that a new competitor can't backfill.
- **The grading data:** every run measures which free signals predict what licensed data later shows. That tells Trace where to spend and where to trust, and it gets better with every portfolio.

## Risks, said plainly
- Funds have small software budgets. That's why we start with the highest-volume portfolios, and why the LP and secondaries buyers matter.
- Data platforms could add a portfolio view. Our answer is speed on the workflow (calls, marks, LP letters) and the grading data.
- 80% agreement means a partner still checks the call list. It's a call list, not a valuation, until accuracy is proven over time.
